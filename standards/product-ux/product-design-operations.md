# Product Design Operations

## Purpose

Ensure product design begins with a real need and produces an evidence-backed, accessible, implementation-ready contract rather than attractive but incomplete screens.

## Expected outcome

The product problem, users, needs, constraints, flows, states, content, accessibility, risks, metrics, and handoff requirements are explicit and verifiable.

## Required lifecycle

`DISCOVER -> DEFINE -> DESIGN -> VALIDATE -> DECISION_LOCKED -> BUILD_READY`

## Required inputs

- Product objective and business context
- Evidence of the user or operational need
- Product types, users, markets, platforms, and constraints
- Known legal, privacy, accessibility, safety, and technical boundaries

## Required actions

- Define actors and verified user needs before creating personas.
- Document primary, alternative, recovery, permission, loading, empty, error, offline, partial, and success states where applicable.
- Use existing design-system components and tokens before creating new ones.
- Evaluate system status, user control, consistency, error prevention, recognition over recall, efficiency, error recovery, and help.
- Validate with representative users or label the work unvalidated.

## Required artifacts

- Product brief
- Assumption log
- Decision log
- User-flow specification
- Journey map or service blueprint when the cross-channel or operational context requires one
- Accessibility requirements
- Design decision record
- Design QA report
- Design handoff contract

## Acceptance criteria

- Need and evidence are stated.
- Scope and non-goals are explicit.
- Primary and recovery paths are complete.
- Interaction states are defined.
- Accessibility requirements are testable.
- Content uses specific language and avoids fake evidence.
- Design tokens and components map to implementation outputs.
- Open assumptions and risks have owners and validation plans.

## Guards

Do not invent research, personas, quotes, metrics, legal applicability, or validation results. “Professional UI” must be translated into measurable hierarchy, typography, spacing, consistency, accessibility, platform convention, content, state coverage, performance, and visual-QA requirements.
