# Standard Authoring Contract

## Purpose

Make every standard usable by humans, AI agents, compilers, reviewers, and auditors without relying on hidden context.

## Expected outcome

A reader can determine when the rule applies, what must be produced, how completion is tested, what evidence is required, and when escalation is mandatory.

## Required sections

Every normative standard must include:

1. Purpose
2. Expected outcome
3. Applicability
4. Non-applicability
5. Required inputs
6. Unknowns to resolve
7. Required actions
8. Required decisions
9. Required details
10. Expected documents and artifacts
11. Document structure
12. Acceptance criteria
13. Verification method
14. Evidence required
15. Failure conditions
16. Risks and abuse cases
17. Guards and limits
18. Exceptions
19. Cost considerations
20. Dependencies
21. Related standards
22. Source provenance
23. Owner and review cycle
24. Zeref execution behavior
25. Examples and anti-patterns

## Requirement vocabulary

- `REQUIRED`: mandatory where applicable.
- `RECOMMENDED`: default unless a documented reason supports another choice.
- `OPTIONAL`: context-dependent improvement.
- `FORBIDDEN`: must not occur.

Do not use `should` to hide whether a rule is mandatory.

## Evidence discipline

Separate facts, assumptions, unknowns, risks, and conflicts. A completed checklist is not evidence by itself. Record the command, file, screenshot, report, source, reviewer, or measurement that proves the outcome.

## Exceptions

An exception requires rule ID, scope, reason, risk, compensating control, owner, approval, review date, and expiry or removal condition.
